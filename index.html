How FORM Fits

Evidence first. Personalization second.

FORM separates product facts, observed performance evidence and our own fitting logic. That lets us explain why something fits you without pretending every data point is equally certain.

01

Identify the golfer.

We collect the minimum useful information about speed, delivery, strike, ball flight, current equipment, preferences and priorities. Exact launch-monitor data improves precision, but it is not required.

02

Describe the product.

FORM stores objective specifications separately from performance traits. Manufacturer-published lofts and dimensions are facts; characteristics such as relative launch, spin and forgiveness require evidence or clearly labeled modeling.

03

Match—not rank.

There is no universal “best driver” or “best ball.” FORM weights the product profile against the golfer profile, their strike pattern and what they ranked as most important.

04

Benchmark what you own.

A recommendation has to beat the golfer’s current equipment by enough to matter. A newer product can score higher without earning an upgrade recommendation.

05

Show confidence.

Recommendation confidence reflects both the quality of golfer inputs and the quality of product evidence. Missing data should lower confidence rather than disappear behind a precise-looking score.

06

Learn from outcomes.

With permission, saved fits can be re-evaluated as the product database changes. FORM should retain recommendation snapshots so meaningful changes are explainable rather than silently overwriting the prior fit. Over time, opt-in anonymized outcomes can also help calibrate which recommendations actually perform better for different golfer profiles.

Data provenance

Not all evidence is the same.

Every important product trait should carry a provenance label so the user can see whether it is a published specification, a FORM inference, or independently validated evidence.

Official specObjective manufacturer-published facts such as loft, head size, length, adjustability, construction and model availability.
FORM modeledOur own normalized interpretation of likely launch, spin, bias, forgiveness, feel or player profile when verified measurements are not yet available.
Independent / licensedPerformance evidence from a source we are permitted to use, a commercial data partnership, published testing with appropriate rights, or FORM’s own testing.
Rules of the model

What FORM will—and won’t—do.

Manufacturer claims

Useful as intended-design context, but marketing language should not automatically become a measured performance fact.

Independent testing

We will not copy or republish proprietary databases. Data will be used only when public use is permitted, licensed, partnered, or generated by FORM.

Historical products

Older clubs can remain in the identification database even when they are no longer eligible for purchase recommendations.

Current recommendations

FORM should recommend current-generation products by default, with previous-generation exceptions only when fit or value makes the case compelling.

Precision

A 92/100 score is not a laboratory measurement. It is an interpretable model output. The interface should always pair scores with a confidence level and evidence quality.

Question burden

FORM should ask a question only when the answer can realistically change the recommendation or confidence. A golfer fitting one category should never be forced to complete an unrelated profile.

Progressive depth

The default experience should provide the shortest credible path to a recommendation. Technical golfers can voluntarily add launch-monitor data and open deeper analysis.

Affiliate revenue follows the fit. It does not determine the fit.

FORM may earn a commission when a user purchases through an eligible link. The recommendation score should be calculated before affiliate availability or commission rate is considered. If the best-fit product has no affiliate program, it should still be allowed to rank first. A retailer fallback can be used for purchase routing without changing the fitting result.

Current prototype status: the equipment attribute layer contains a mix of official product identification data and FORM-modeled performance traits. Independent performance evidence has not yet been fully licensed or calibrated into the prototype. The production interface should expose this distinction exactly as shown above.

Communication standard

Silence is part of the product.

FORM should not manufacture reasons to contact a golfer. After the initial fit follow-up, a product email should require a meaningful personalized signal. If nothing important changes, FORM should send nothing.

Initial fit windowA limited number of useful follow-ups can help the golfer finish evaluating the original recommendation. They should add information, not simply repeat the result.
After the initial windowNew-product messages require a meaningful fit change, not merely a product launch or a retailer promotion.
Frequency guardrailRepeated marginal changes are suppressed or bundled. After the initial fit window, silence is the default unless FORM has a genuinely meaningful personalized update.
